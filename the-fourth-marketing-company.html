The Fourth Marketing Company in a Year and a Half

On a recorded call in August, a client told us exactly where we sat in her history of hiring help. She let us share the clip. It is not a testimonial, and that is why it is worth listening to.

Near the end of a long call on 18 August 2026, Rebekah Severi of Freccia Construction said something that stopped the conversation. She was explaining why she needed to check in with her brother and her husband before committing to anything, and the reason turned out to be simple arithmetic.

Rebekah Severi
Freccia Construction, Austin, Texas

Recorded on a client call and published with her permission. The section where she named the previous companies has been removed at our own request, because this is not about them.

What she said

"Mind you, you guys are the fourth marketing team in a year and a half. So we have some really good friends that we were working with. They did really good work for our old business, but for some reason it was not translating to this new business, and they could not figure it out."

Section removed, the previous companies named

"So I don't care where the good marketing is found. [You guys] have been great in getting the new website that's actually working for us and not working against us. All of the steps we've done are awesome."

Why We Are Publishing This

The obvious move is to clip the last six seconds. All of the steps we've done are awesome, five stars, on to the next post. We are not doing that, because the first part is the part that actually helps someone reading this.

If you run a business and you are looking for marketing help, there is a good chance you have already paid someone who did not work out. That experience tends to come with a quiet suspicion that the problem might be you. It usually is not. Rebekah went through three attempts in eighteen months, and she is sharp, decisive, and runs a business that is growing. Hiring the wrong help repeatedly is not evidence of bad judgement. It is evidence that this is genuinely hard to get right.

"They did really good work for our old business, but for some reason it was not translating to this new business, and they could not figure it out."

Rebekah Severi, Freccia Construction

The Failure Nobody Names

Read that line again, because it describes the most common way this goes wrong, and it has nothing to do with skill.

The team before her current one were friends. They were good. They had proved it on a different business she was involved in. Then they took the same approach to a custom construction company in Austin and it did not land, and they could not work out why.

That is not laziness. It is what happens when a team brings a method to a business instead of building a method for it. Construction is not retail. A custom builder is not a contractor. Someone choosing who will build their house is making a decision they will live inside for thirty years, and they research it like it. The channels that work for a fast purchase do very little for that, and the people running those channels often never find out, because the reporting still looks fine.

What We Did Differently, Honestly

We would like to say we had a brilliant insight. We did not. We spent the early weeks asking questions and staying quiet, which is less impressive and more useful.

The thing she singles out in the clip is the website, and specifically that it is working for the business rather than against it. That is a low bar to clear on paper and a surprisingly common place to fail. A site can look excellent and still work against you, by loading slowly, by burying the work that wins jobs, by being invisible to the searches your buyers actually type, or by presenting a custom builder as though they were a general contractor. Fixing that was not creative. It was reading the business properly first.

Being the fourth is not a compliment. It means three teams already had this chance and the client is out of patience. The only reasonable response is to assume you are on trial, because you are.

The Bar This Sets

There is a version of this page that ends with a flourish about partnership. Here is the more honest ending.

Rebekah was clear on the same call about how she measures anything: whether it brings in work. Not impressions, not followers, not how good the reporting looks. She was pleasant about it and completely unmoveable, and she is right to be. Every previous team presumably also had good months of activity to show her.

So the standard we are held to here is the correct one, and we are publishing this partly so that it stays that way in public. If the work stops producing, being the friendly team who answers quickly will not save us, and it should not.

If you are about to hire your third or fourth

  • Judge the first month by the questions they ask, not by how fast they show you something pretty.
  • Be suspicious of a team that has never worked in anything like your industry and is not curious about it.
  • Ask what they still do not understand about your business. A vague answer means they have not looked.
  • Agree up front on the one number that decides whether this is working, and make it a business number.
  • Tell them plainly that they are not the first. A team worth hiring will find that useful rather than awkward.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why publish that a client hired three others first?

Because it is the most useful thing a prospective client can hear. Most owners looking for help have already been let down, and pretending otherwise helps nobody.

Did the client agree to this being shared?

Yes, asked and answered on the same recorded call, with the agreement that any mention of the previous companies by name would be cut. Those sections are removed from the clip, and no former agency is named anywhere on this page.

What actually goes wrong with agencies that do not work out?

Usually not incompetence. The work is fine in isolation and wrong for the specific business, because nobody spent long enough understanding what this business sells and to whom.

How do I avoid hiring a fourth?

Ask what they still do not know about you. See whether the answer is specific. See the checklist above.
